Read this before you apply

Philosophy

This is how the Lab works. Read it once. Then you know what you are walking into.

Start here

If this is your first time, do it in this order.

  1. Decide what you want to make, and why. One sentence each is enough to begin our project file. You can change it later and you will be adding.
  2. Apply. You send that plan. If it is not ready, it comes back with a reason. You change it and send it again. An excellent plan might pass the first time, but its very rare. The form is not open yet. Until the truck is built, this page is the prep.
  3. When the truck is in town, look around for the first hour. Ask Miko if you have a question. She is the digital facilitator. She helps you get clear on what you are making and the next step. You do the making.
  4. Pay at the door. $1 an hour, cash, for the hours you booked.
  5. Then you work. You make it, and you take it home. If you break something, you help fix it or assist fixing it.

What MobileLab is

A 53-ft shop on a truck. It visits small towns across the rural great plains. This is facility #12 in a 14-year research program. The first 11 were buildings. This one travels. Ages 7 to 77. Age is not really a barrier, responsibility is. A responsible 7 years old has more priority tham a an irresponsible 50 year old.

Everyone here is a creator. You are not a student, and you are not a customer buying a finished thing. One rule: be responsible. Under that sit about 100 best practices. They are not rules. Each one makes the learning better. Missing one is not a punishment.

What it is not

  • Not a school. No teacher, no course you must finish, no grade from us. It is harder than school, because you have to make yourself do it. The only lesson you must pass is a short safety tutorial for a machine you choose to run. You can do that tutorial before the truck arrives.
  • Not a service. The $1 does not hire someone to make your project. It is the price of walking in. You hand over cash because you want the time.
  • Not a contest. Nobody ranks what you made. There is no top creator. The only person you are up against is you.
  • Not a business. Not a money making venture, all incoming funds go to development and operational costs.
  • Not a charity. We do not follow charity rules of regulations. We choose what is best for the kids individual learning, not adults trying to regulate or control.

Nobody is forced

You come because you decided to. No one here can make you finish, stay, or take their idea. Almost all of what you get done is you making yourself do it.

A facilitator, including Miko, points you at the next step and then leaves you to it. An accelerator is the person who can sign you off on a machine. They give advice only when you ask. You can say no. Stopping halfway is allowed. Making a different small thing on each machine is allowed. You do not owe one finished object.

Small towns first

The truck stops in towns under 1,000 people. A city usually already have these machines. If no small town has asked, and people in a city want the shop, the truck can go there. Small towns first. Not small towns only.

You against you

The truck brings the tools to people who cannot get them otherwise, and keeps the price low so money is not what stops you. What is left is your own mind. You are practicing how to learn. There is no scoreboard. You just have to do a little better than the day before. You acknowledge your improvement.

What you pay

Everyone pays $1 an hour in coins or bills at the door, each day they walk in. Most people bring $4 for four hours in the shop. You are encouraged to bring you own materials. You can also use material from the truck's shelves, you pay for that separately, at cost, through Miko or this website. A parent can pay that part while you are on the floor. Material you bring yourself is not billed. The dollar is there so this place matches the real world: things cost something, there are no free lunches.

How you get time

You do not just pick a clock time. You apply with what you will make and why. If the plan is not ready, it comes back with a reason, and you send it again. School often gives you one try. You keep trying to until you get to some success.

About the Lab · not a show to watch

About

An unmarked white reefer. Outer doors, a steep ramp, then the lab door. Enter your secret code.

You pay at the door, in coins or bills: $1 for each hour booked that day. A 4-hour shop shift is $4. A pit hour is $1. You pay again every day you walk in. Facility stock is extra, paid by you or the booker through Miko or the website. Bring your own materials if you want. Those are not billed.

Day 1 starts with a look-around. Creators and parents of anyone under 18 walk the truck. Max or Miko answers questions. By the end of that hour, creators are booked in with Miko (kiosk video, kid alone) and everyone else is out. After that, the kid is on the floor alone.

Pit first: four seats on one shared bench, level 0, no powered machine. Shop is 4 to 12 hours on the raised deck.

One rule: be responsible. About 100 best practices sit under it. None of them are rules. Each one makes the room better for learning. Practice them and you are optimizing the work.

You make it, you take it. You break it, you help fix it. Leave scrap for the pit if you want. A plan that is not ready comes back with a reason. A tool-tour is a valid plan.

FAQ

Can I book?

Not yet. Creator dashboard opens when the truck is funded and built.

Where is the truck right now?

It isn’t moving. The map is the intended area, not a tracker. There will never be a public live GPS pin.

Is it a school?

No. See Philosophy.

What does it cost?

Everyone pays $1 an hour, cash at the door, each day you walk in — coins or bills. Most people bring $4 for a 4-hour shop shift. Pit-hour is $1. If you want facility stock, you or the booker pay through Miko or the website. Bring your own if you want — that part is not billed.

Is it safe?

Real tools, managed risk. This is a medium danger facility. Homes, schools, public centres etc. are all low danger. However the real world is high danger. This is like boot camp for real world danger and responsibility practice.

What does a host town provide?

A place to park, shore power, water/sewer hookups. Email for the host-site forms package when you are ready to host.

How do I help?

Donate. Or register interest to create or to host. One email. No newsletter.
